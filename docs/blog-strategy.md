# MigRent Blog Strategy

Editorial, UX, and content plan for the MigRent blog. People-first, launch-safe, and clearly distinct from the Help Center and Guides.

Last updated: 2026-05-09

---

## SECTION 1 - BLOG STRATEGY

The blog is MigRent's editorial voice. Help Center and Guides answer "how do I do X?" The blog answers "what is actually going on, what should I expect, and what do real people in this situation need to know?"

What the blog should do that guides/help center should not:
- Tell the truth about the rental experience in Australia for newcomers, including what is uncomfortable, confusing, or unfair.
- Set expectations before someone is mid-task. Guides help in the moment. Blog posts prepare and orient.
- Add context, narrative, and lived experience. A guide is a checklist. A blog post is a perspective.
- Cover broader life topics (settling, suburbs, culture, money habits) that are useful but do not belong in product docs.

How blog content builds trust:
- Specific, accurate, Australia-only information. No US/UK-flavoured generic advice.
- Honest tone. Acknowledge what is hard, what is unfair, and what newcomers usually get wrong.
- Real numbers and real examples (rent ranges, bond amounts, typical fees) updated yearly.
- A consistent editorial voice that sounds like a knowledgeable friend, not a brand.
- Clear "Updated on" dates and a named editorial team.

How to avoid generic content marketing:
- Every post must answer: "Would a migrant or student forward this to a friend?" If no, do not publish.
- No keyword-stuffed listicles. No "Top 10 reasons to rent in Sydney."
- Each post must contain at least one thing the reader cannot easily find elsewhere: a real number, a non-obvious tip, a local nuance, a step most sites skip.
- Avoid generic intros ("Australia is a beautiful country..."). Start with the reader's actual problem.

How to be useful without being repetitive:
- The blog explains the why and the context. Guides explain the how. Help Center explains the what.
- Blog posts can link out to a guide for the step-by-step, instead of repeating it.
- Each blog post focuses on one decision, one misconception, or one piece of context.

How the blog supports the brand:
- Reinforces MigRent as the rental platform that actually understands migrants and students.
- Signals competence and care without being self-promotional.
- Gives organic reasons to land on MigRent that are not transactional, which builds long-term trust.

## SECTION 2 - BLOG VS GUIDES VS HELP CENTER VS LEGAL

| Surface | Purpose | Tone | Content type | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blog | Orient, contextualise, build trust | Editorial, warm, human, opinionated within reason | Explainers, stories, myth-vs-reality, suburb context, money realities | 800-1,800 words |
| Guides | Walk a user through a real task, end to end | Instructional, calm, structured | Step-by-step processes, checklists, templated emails | 600-1,500 words |
| Help Center | Answer one specific product or process question fast | Direct, factual, short | FAQs, troubleshooting, policy explanations | 80-300 words |
| Legal/Info | Communicate binding terms, policies, and disclosures | Formal, precise, neutral | Terms, Privacy, Refunds, Eligibility | As required |

Where overlap is okay:
- A blog post can summarise a topic and link to the guide for the steps.
- A guide can link to a help article for an edge-case answer.
- A help article can link out to a blog post for "why this works this way."

Where overlap becomes confusing:
- Two pages with the same title across surfaces. Pick one home.
- A blog post that is actually a step-by-step guide. Move it.
- A help article giving opinions or context. Strip it back to the answer.
- Anything in the blog that should legally be in Terms or Refund Policy. Remove it.

Decision rule:
- If the user is mid-task and wants steps -> Guide.
- If the user has one specific question or error -> Help Center.
- If the user is curious, comparing, or trying to understand -> Blog.
- If it is a binding rule, policy, or disclosure -> Legal.

## SECTION 3 - CONTENT PILLARS

Five pillars, ranked by priority for migrants and students.

1. Renting in Australia, Explained (highest priority)
   - How leases, bonds, agents, condition reports, and rent increases actually work.
   - The system newcomers must learn before they can navigate it.

2. Newcomer Realities and Mistakes
   - What catches people off guard: rental ledgers, 100-point ID, references, viewings, application competition.
   - Honest content about what is harder for migrants and students.

3. Safety, Scams, and Red Flags
   - Marketplace scams, fake listings, deposit fraud, sublet traps, dodgy operators, off-platform pressure.
   - High value, evergreen, and brand-aligned with MigRent's safety positioning.

4. Cost, Budget, and Sharing
   - Realistic monthly costs, room-share economics, utilities, internet, transport, hidden fees.
   - Translates into better decisions on day one.

5. Settling In and Local Context
   - Suburb explainers, transport, groceries, halal/vegetarian/student-friendly areas, banking, phone plans, GP/Medicare basics as they relate to housing.
   - Useful for the human, not just the tenant.

Lower priority (only after the top 5 are mature):
- Stories and case studies from real migrants and students.
- Macro/trend posts: rental market context, vacancy rates, what is changing in policy.

## SECTION 4 - ARTICLE TYPES

Recommended formats, with launch safety:

1. Explainers (SAFE, START HERE)
   - "How rental applications actually work in Australia."
   - Best for orientation. Hardest to mess up. Highest evergreen value.

2. Myth vs Reality (SAFE)
   - "5 things newcomers think about Aussie rentals that are wrong."
   - Engaging, honest, brand-aligned. Easy to write well.

3. Comparisons (SAFE)
   - "Sharehouse vs studio vs student accommodation: real costs."
   - High decision-value. Use real ranges, update annually.

4. Practical Opinion-Backed (SAFE WITH CARE)
   - "Why we think 'no pets, no kids, no students' is a red flag."
   - Builds voice and trust. Keep the opinion grounded, not preachy.

5. Curated Resources (SAFE, LIGHT EFFORT)
   - "The only 6 government links a new renter actually needs."
   - High utility, low writing burden, easy to keep updated.

6. Suburb / Lifestyle Posts (MEDIUM)
   - "Living in Footscray as a student: what to actually expect."
   - Strong fit for audience. Needs local accuracy, so do not over-publish.

7. Stories / Case Studies (MEDIUM, LATER)
   - "How Priya found a sharehouse in Sydney in 11 days."
   - Powerful for trust but requires real interviews and consent. Defer to V2.

8. Trend / Context Posts (RISKIER, LATER)
   - "What the latest CoreLogic vacancy data means for renters."
   - Goes stale fast. Save for when you have editorial bandwidth.

9. Legal-Adjacent Posts (DO NOT DO ALONE)
   - Anything tenancy-law-specific. Always frame as "general info, check your state body" and link to the official source.

Launch-safe set: Explainers, Myth-vs-Reality, Comparisons, Practical Opinion, Curated Resources.

## SECTION 6 - ARTICLE STRUCTURE (REPEATABLE TEMPLATE)

```
1. Title
   Specific, plain-English, no clickbait. Promises one clear thing.

2. Subtitle / Dek (1 sentence)
   Who this is for and what they will get out of it.

3. Meta row
   Author or "MigRent Editorial", updated date, reading time, category, tags.

4. Hook (2-4 short sentences)
   Start with the reader's real situation, not with Australia in general.

5. Why this matters (3-5 sentences)
   The cost of getting this wrong, or the upside of getting it right.

6. Core explanation (the body)
   Use H2 sections. Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
   Use lists when there are 3+ parallel items.
   Use real numbers, real ranges, real examples.

7. Practical takeaways (Key Takeaways Box)
   3-6 bullets. Plain, scannable, action-oriented.

8. Optional: a real example or mini case
   Anonymised. One short paragraph.

9. Related guides and help articles
   2-4 links to deeper how-tos and FAQs.

10. Disclaimer (only when needed)
    "General information, not legal advice. For tenancy law, contact [state body]."

11. Updated date and editorial note
    "Reviewed by MigRent Editorial on [date]. We update this post yearly."
```

## SECTION 7 - WRITING STYLE

Tone:
- Warm, plain, confident. Sound like a knowledgeable friend who has done this before.
- Honest about what is hard. Avoid sugar-coating the rental market.
- Not corporate, not gimmicky, not legalistic.
- No emojis in body copy. No exclamation marks except in real quotes.

Reader:
- Assume English is a second language for many readers. Aim for Year 9 reading level.
- Avoid idioms ("get the lay of the land", "skin in the game"). Use direct words.
- Define Australian-specific terms on first use (bond, ledger, REA, condition report).

Paragraph rules:
- 1-3 sentences per paragraph. Almost never longer.
- Lead each section with the answer, then the explanation.
- One idea per paragraph.

Heading rules:
- H2 for sections, H3 for sub-points only when needed.
- Headings are descriptive, not cute. "How bond works" beats "The deposit dance."
- Use sentence case, not title case.

Lists:
- Use lists for 3 or more parallel items.
- Keep list items the same shape (all start with verbs, or all are nouns).
- Avoid lists of 1-2 items. Just write a sentence.

Examples:
- Use real numbers and real cities. "A studio in Footscray is around $380-$450/week" beats "studios are affordable."
- Anonymise people. "Priya, an international student in Sydney" is fine. No surnames, no agents named.

Readability:
- Default to short words. "Use" not "utilise." "Show" not "demonstrate."
- Cut filler: "in order to", "at this point in time", "it is important to note that."
- Avoid passive voice unless it is clearer.

Self-promotion:
- Do not start posts talking about MigRent.
- Mention MigRent only where it genuinely helps the reader, and at most twice per post.
- Never end with a hard CTA. A soft contextual link is fine.

Australian English:
- "Apartment", "rent", "bond", "agent", "lease", "tenant", "landlord."
- "Organise", "centre", "favourite", "colour."
- No em dashes anywhere. Use a regular dash with spaces, or a comma, or a full stop.

## SECTION 8 - VISUAL AND UX DESIGN

Layout:
- Single-column reading area, max width about 680-720px on desktop.
- Generous line height (about 1.7) and 17-18px body text.
- Strong vertical rhythm: clear space above H2s.
- Sticky right rail (desktop only) with table of contents and reading progress.

Typography:
- One serif or humanist-sans for body, one sans for UI and headings. Two fonts max.
- Tight headings, comfortable body. Avoid all-caps for anything beyond tags.
- Italics only for emphasis or quoted phrases, not decoration.

Hierarchy:
- Hero block: category tag, title, subtitle, meta row, optional hero image.
- Body: H2 sections separated by extra space, optional pull quotes.
- Footer block: key takeaways, related posts, editorial note.

Color:
- Use one accent colour from the MigRent palette for category tags and link underlines.
- Callouts use soft background tints, not loud fills.
- Keep contrast WCAG AA at minimum.

Callouts (use sparingly):
- Tip (light blue): a non-obvious helpful nudge.
- Warning (amber): scams, fees, irreversible actions.
- Note (neutral grey): definitions or context.
- Key Takeaways (subtle accent): always at the end of every post.

Other elements:
- Reading time badge in the meta row.
- Progress bar on scroll, top of page, thin and subtle.
- Inline images: full-width within the reading column, captioned, lazy-loaded.
- Related posts: 3 cards, same category preferred.
- Tag chips link to a tag index page.

Mobile:
- Full-width reading column with 20px side padding.
- Sticky compact meta on scroll (title + reading time).
- No right rail. Move TOC into a collapsible at the top.
- Tap targets at least 44px.

Editorial feel:
- White space. Restraint. Don't decorate every paragraph.
- Real photos over stock photos. If using stock, pick Australian-looking imagery.
- Author block at the end, not the top, to keep the focus on the reader's problem first.

## SECTION 9 - FRONTEND IMPLEMENTATION (Next.js + Tailwind + shadcn)

Route structure (current pages router setup):
```
frontend/pages/blog/
  index.tsx                  // Blog landing
  [slug].tsx                 // Article page
  category/[category].tsx    // Category index (new)
  tag/[tag].tsx              // Tag index (new)
```

If/when migrating to the app router later:
```
app/blog/page.tsx
app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx
app/blog/category/[category]/page.tsx
app/blog/tag/[tag]/page.tsx
```

Reusable components (place under `frontend/components/blog/`):
- `BlogHero.tsx` - title, subtitle, hero image, category tag, meta row.
- `ArticleMeta.tsx` - author, updated date, reading time, category.
- `TopicTag.tsx` - small chip linking to /blog/tag/[tag].
- `KeyTakeawaysBox.tsx` - end-of-article callout with 3-6 bullets.
- `RelatedPosts.tsx` - 3 cards, picks same category first.
- `ReadingTimeBadge.tsx` - calculated from word count.
- `EditorialNote.tsx` - "Reviewed by MigRent Editorial on [date]" block.
- `ResourceLinks.tsx` - curated outbound link list with descriptions.
- `Callout.tsx` - variants: tip, warning, note.
- `TableOfContents.tsx` - auto-generated from H2s, sticky on desktop.
- `ReadingProgress.tsx` - thin scroll-position bar at top.
- `ArticleCard.tsx` - used on index, category, tag pages.
- `BlogCategoryNav.tsx` - top bar with the 5 pillars.
- `Disclaimer.tsx` - reusable "general info, not legal advice" block.

Design consistency with Guides and Help Center:
- Share the same shell, header, and footer across all three.
- Share `Callout`, `KeyTakeawaysBox`, `Disclaimer`, and `RelatedPosts` components.
- Differentiate by:
  - Blog: serif headings or a distinct heading family, editorial hero, byline visible.
  - Guides: stepper UI, numbered sections, "Next step" CTA at the bottom.
  - Help Center: compact list view, search-first UI, FAQ accordion style.
- Use a single design tokens file (Tailwind config) so spacing, color, and type stay in sync.

Content source:
- For V1, MDX files in `frontend/content/blog/` is fine and lowest-risk.
- Or store posts in Supabase (`blog_posts` table) and render via getStaticProps with revalidate.
- Either way, do not introduce a new CMS for V1.

## SECTION 10 - EDITORIAL GOVERNANCE

Roles:
- Editorial owner: one person who approves what ships.
- Writer: drafts the post.
- Reviewer: fact-checks and runs the QA checklist.

Workflow:
1. Topic intake. Anyone can suggest a topic. Editorial owner accepts or declines weekly.
2. Brief. Before writing, the editor fills a one-page brief: reader, problem, takeaway, sources, related guides.
3. Draft. Writer drafts in Google Docs or Notion using the article template.
4. Review. Reviewer runs the QA checklist (Section 12). Editor approves.
5. Publish. Set updated date. Add to category and tag indexes. Add 2-4 internal links.
6. Review cycle. Every post gets re-reviewed once a year, or sooner if rules/numbers change.

Choosing topics:
- Source from real questions in support tickets, Help Center search logs, and onboarding feedback.
- Reject topics that exist mainly for SEO traffic with no real reader problem.
- Prefer topics where MigRent has a credible, useful angle.

Avoiding duplication:
- Maintain a single content map (Notion or a markdown table in `docs/`) listing every Help Center article, Guide, and Blog post.
- Before commissioning a post, search the map. If it exists as a Help/Guide, ask whether the blog adds context, not steps.

Keeping content updated:
- Posts with prices or law references get an annual review (every July, end of FY).
- Posts about scams or market context get reviewed every 6 months.
- Mark stale posts with a banner if not updated within 18 months.

When linking:
- Each blog post links to at least 1 guide and 1 help article where natural.
- Each guide links back to at most 1 contextual blog post.
- Help Center articles link to blog posts only when extra context truly helps.

Blog vs Guide vs FAQ decision:
- Single specific question, short answer -> Help/FAQ.
- Multi-step task with a clear end state -> Guide.
- Context, decision, or "what should I expect" -> Blog.

Avoiding thin content:
- Minimum 700 words for a published blog post. If a topic cannot meet this honestly, it belongs in Help.
- Every post must have a Key Takeaways box and at least 3 internal links.
- Every post must include at least one specific, verifiable claim (a number, a process, a state body name).

## SECTION 12 - QA CHECKLIST

Before publishing any blog post, every box must be ticked:

Usefulness
- [ ] Solves a real problem a migrant or student would search for or ask.
- [ ] Contains at least one specific, non-obvious piece of information.
- [ ] Would I forward this to a friend in this situation?

Originality
- [ ] Does not repeat an existing Help Center article or Guide.
- [ ] Adds context, decision-making, or perspective beyond steps.

Voice
- [ ] No corporate or marketing tone.
- [ ] No fluffy intro about Australia in general.
- [ ] No em dashes. No emojis in body. No exclamation marks (except quotes).
- [ ] Reads at roughly Year 9 level. Defines all Aussie-specific terms.

Structure
- [ ] Title is specific and plain.
- [ ] Subtitle states who it is for and what they get.
- [ ] Has Why this matters, Body, Key Takeaways, Related links.
- [ ] At least 700 words and at most 1,800.

Accuracy
- [ ] Numbers and ranges are sourced and dated.
- [ ] Any law or policy reference links to the official state body.
- [ ] Disclaimer present where needed.

Linking
- [ ] At least 1 link to a guide and 1 to a help article.
- [ ] At most 1 contextual mention of MigRent.
- [ ] All outbound links open in a new tab and are alive (no 404s).

Visual
- [ ] Hero block, meta row, and reading time render correctly.
- [ ] Headings are H2 with one H1 (the title).
- [ ] No paragraph longer than 4 sentences.
- [ ] Images have alt text and load quickly.

Mobile
- [ ] Reads cleanly on a phone with 20px side padding.
- [ ] Tap targets are 44px+.
- [ ] TOC collapses on mobile.

Hygiene
- [ ] Updated date is correct.
- [ ] Category and tags are set and exist on the index pages.
- [ ] Old/duplicate version is unpublished or redirected.
- [ ] Editorial note signed off by reviewer.

### 3. Content decision framework (blog vs guide vs help center)

Use this short flow whenever a topic comes in:

1. Is the user mid-task and needs steps to a clear end state?
   - YES -> Guide.
   - NO -> go to 2.

2. Is it a single specific question with a short factual answer?
   - YES -> Help Center / FAQ.
   - NO -> go to 3.

3. Is the answer mostly context, decisions, expectations, or perspective?
   - YES -> Blog.
   - NO -> go to 4.

4. Is it a binding policy, term, refund rule, or legal disclosure?
   - YES -> Legal/Info page. Do not put in blog or help.
   - NO -> reject the topic; it is probably too thin to publish.

Quick test: if you can answer it in under 200 words with no opinion, it is Help. If it has steps that produce an outcome, it is a Guide. If it teaches the reader to think about the problem, it is a Blog post.

### 4. Biggest blog mistakes to remove from the current site

1. Generic intros. Cut anything that starts with "Australia is a vibrant country..." Lead with the reader's actual problem.
2. SEO listicles ("Top 10 best suburbs"). Replace with explainers or comparisons that have a clear takeaway.
3. Posts that duplicate Help Center content. Either delete or rewrite to add real context.
4. Self-promotional fluff about MigRent. Remove brand-led framing from the first 3 paragraphs of every post.
5. Walls of text. Break paragraphs to 1-3 sentences and add subheadings.
6. Vague advice. Replace with real numbers, ranges, and named state bodies.
7. Stale posts with no updated date. Add a visible "Updated on" or unpublish.
8. Missing Key Takeaways. Add a box at the end of every post.
9. Em dashes throughout. Replace with regular dashes, commas, or full stops.
10. Stock-feeling photos with no Australian context. Replace or remove.
11. CTAs at the top ("Sign up to MigRent"). Move to a contextual mention only.
12. Posts under 500 words pretending to be articles. Move to Help or expand.
13. Legal-flavoured content without a disclaimer or source link. Add both, or rewrite.
14. No related links at the bottom. Add 3 every time.
15. Inconsistent tone across posts. Run every post through the Section 7 style rules.
